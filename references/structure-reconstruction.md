# Structure Reconstruction

Structure reconstruction is mandatory. Complete it before evaluation.

## Required Reconstruction Fields

Identify:

- Title.
- Material type.
- Review mode.
- Field.
- Research question.
- Thesis or central claim.
- Argument map.
- Section, chapter, or paragraph structure.
- Main international law materials.
- Evidence limits.

## Method

1. Extract the title, headings, abstract, introduction, conclusion, and any roadmap.
2. Identify the research question from explicit statements first; if it is implied, state that it is reconstructed.
3. Identify the thesis from the introduction, conclusion, and repeated claims.
4. Build an argument map with no more than six major moves unless the paper is unusually complex.
5. List the main legal materials and classify their legal role.
6. State evidence limits caused by partial material, extraction quality, missing pages, missing footnotes, or unclear source data.

Do not output standalone strongest-element or weakest-element subsections by default. If a strength or weakness materially affects the professor's assessment, fold it into the overall judgment, core defect snapshot, or main problems section.

## Argument Map Format

Use this format:

```markdown
### Argument Map

1. The paper begins from [problem or controversy].
2. It claims that [central claim].
3. It supports that claim by [first analytical move].
4. It then relies on [second analytical move].
5. It addresses or fails to address [counterpoint, authority, or limitation].
6. It concludes that [legal or scholarly implication].
```

## Evidence Limits Language

Use direct language:

- "This is a review of the submitted abstract, not of the full paper."
- "Because the submitted material lacks footnotes, citation quality can only be assessed at the level of source selection and legal framing."
- "Because page references were not available from extraction, location references use headings and paragraphs instead."
- "The conclusion below should not be read as a judgment on sections not submitted."

## Prohibition

Do not begin with praise, criticism, grading, or publication judgment before the reconstruction is complete.
