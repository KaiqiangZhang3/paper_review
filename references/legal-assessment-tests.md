# Legal Assessment Tests

Use these tests to make the review more legally diagnostic. Apply them after structure reconstruction and before writing the final memorandum.

## Three-Layer Legal Assessment

For each core claim, distinguish:

1. Lex lata: does existing law support the claim?
2. Lex ferenda or normative theory: is the claim defensible as a proposal?
3. Academic standard: is the claim acceptable for the submitted academic level and review mode?

Do not allow the paper to use normative desirability as if it were positive law.

## Claim-Strength Calibration

Check whether the paper's conclusion is stronger than its evidence permits.

Use this scale:

1. Descriptive claim: `X has occurred`.
2. Interpretive claim: `X should be read as Y`.
3. Weight claim: `courts or institutions should consider X`.
4. Presumptive claim: `courts or institutions should normally follow X unless rebutted`.
5. Binding claim: `courts or institutions must follow X`.

If the paper argues at level 4 or 5 but proves only level 2 or 3, identify this as a major defect.

## Adverse-Authority Stress Test

When counterargument review is requested, identify the strongest objection to the thesis, not merely objections the paper already mentions.

For each central claim, ask:

- What authority cuts against it?
- What institutional design concern does it ignore?
- What alternative explanation fits the same evidence?
- What would a skeptical court, journal reviewer, or examiner say?
- Does the paper answer that objection directly, evade it, or fail to see it?

For full papers with counterargument review requested, actively look for at least 5 adverse points or explain why fewer are visible from the submitted material.

## Comparative Transfer Check

When a paper uses domestic law or comparative law to support international law claims, assess:

- What is being transferred: rule, analogy, institutional logic, or normative vocabulary.
- Whether the domestic rule depends on constitutional structure, statute, democratic accountability, judicial review design, or separation of powers.
- Whether the international institution has functional equivalents.
- What differences block transfer.
- Whether the paper states the transfer limits.

Treat unsupported domestic-to-international transfer as a serious reasoning defect.

## Institution Differentiation Rule

Do not treat different international bodies as interchangeable.

When the paper discusses international bodies, distinguish:

- Compliance committees.
- COP/MOP decisions.
- Expert committees.
- Human rights treaty bodies.
- Technical bodies.
- International courts and tribunals.

For each institution, identify when relevant:

- Legal basis.
- Composition.
- Mandate.
- Procedure.
- Participation rules.
- Binding or non-binding output.
- Review or appeal mechanism.
