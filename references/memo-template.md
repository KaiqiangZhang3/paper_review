# Internal Review Memorandum Template

Use this template for the professor-facing output. The memo should be direct and analytical. It should not soften defects for student-facing delivery unless the professor separately asks for that version.

Use the professor's chosen output language from intake question 4 for the whole memorandum, including headings and the saved Markdown file. Translate the template headings into that language rather than copying the English headings mechanically.

## Template

```markdown
# [Localized equivalent of "Review Memorandum"]: [Paper Title or File Name]

## [Localized equivalent of "Basic Information"]

- [Review mode]: [master's paper / doctoral paper / peer-review manuscript / other]
- [Material reviewed]: [full paper / chapter / abstract / outline / research proposal / other]
- [Field]: [public international law / international economic law / international criminal law / mixed]
- [Rubric or special standard]: [none supplied / supplied and considered]
- [Citation review requested]: [yes / no]
- [Counterargument and limits review requested]: [yes / no]

## [Localized equivalent of "Structure Reconstruction"]

### [Research Question]

[State the research question. If reconstructed, say so.]

### [Thesis or Central Claim]

[State the thesis or explain that no stable thesis is visible.]

### [Argument Map]

1. [Major move one.]
2. [Major move two.]
3. [Major move three.]
4. [Major move four.]

### [Structure]

[Describe the section or chapter structure and whether it serves the argument.]

### [Main International Law Materials]

[List the treaties, customary law materials, cases, state practice, opinio juris evidence, institutional materials, soft law, scholarship, or field-specific materials used.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Overall Judgment"]

[Give a concise professor-facing judgment. Do not assign a score or grade unless requested. Include claim-strength calibration here when the paper's central claim overstates or understates the evidence. Mention real strengths only when they are analytically important; do not create a separate strengths section by default.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Core Defect Snapshot"]

[Three to five direct sentences identifying the deepest defects. Do not include praise here. Include whether each deep defect is fatal, serious but reparable, or minor when that classification is useful.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Main Problems"]

[For full papers, identify multiple distinct diagnostic problems without padding the memo with trivial issues. Each problem should begin with the most precise available location reference: page, section, heading, paragraph number, footnote number, or extracted paragraph marker. If no precise location is available, say why and use the nearest heading or section.]

## [Localized equivalent of "International Law Materials and Citation Quality"]

[Assess legal sources, source hierarchy, citation support, and suspicious legal propositions. If citation review was not requested, limit this section to issues visible from the main text.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Citation Audit"]

[Include this section when citation review was requested. Classify concrete citation issues using `citation-audit.md`. For full papers, actively look for 10 concrete issues or explain why fewer are visible.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Mode-Specific Assessment"]

[Apply the selected master's, doctoral, or peer-review standard.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Thesis-Specific Assessment"] (For Doctoral Work Only)

[Include this section ONLY for doctoral dissertations and thesis chapters. Omit for master's papers and peer-review manuscripts unless they claim major theoretical contribution.]

### [Problem Scope and Scale]

[Does the thesis clearly define what problem it addresses, at what level of significance, and with what boundaries? Is the problem scope appropriate for a doctoral thesis?]

### [Methodological Path and Alternatives]

[Does the thesis identify its analytical framework (doctrinal, theoretical, empirical, normative, institutional)? Does it acknowledge alternative approaches and justify its choice?]

### [Contribution and Challenge]

[Does the thesis explicitly state what it contributes (new interpretation, theoretical framework, empirical finding, normative critique) or challenges (dominant scholarly position, received doctrine, policy consensus)? Is this contribution demonstrated?]

### [Adverse Scholarship Engagement]

[Does the thesis identify scholarship that challenges its claims? Does it respond substantively in analytical chapters, or only cite contrary views in a disconnected literature review?]

## [Localized equivalent of "Writing and Structure"]

[Assess organization, headings, paragraph logic, roadmap, and clarity.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Adverse Authority and Counterargument Stress Test"]

[Include this section when counterargument review was requested. Identify the strongest objections, adverse authorities, institutional design concerns, alternative explanations, and whether the paper answers them.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Conclusion"]

[State what the professor should take away from the review. Do not create a priority repair checklist unless requested.]

## [Localized equivalent of "Evidence Limits"]

[State limits caused by partial material, extraction, missing pages, missing footnotes, or unavailable source data.]
```

## Length Rule

Write only as much as the paper's problems require. Serious or complex problems justify a longer memo. A strong or straightforward paper should receive a concise memo.

For full-paper review memoranda, default to a compact professor-facing version approximately 15% shorter than an exhaustive internal memorandum. Preserve accuracy, concrete locations, citation diagnoses, and adverse-authority analysis by merging overlapping points, cutting generic praise, and avoiding repeated explanations across sections.

## Tone Rule

Use direct professional judgment. For student papers, identify defects clearly and include improvement direction when useful. Do not generate a student-facing message by default.

## Default Section Rule

Do not include standalone sections titled Claim-Strength Calibration, Main Strengths, Strongest Element, or Weakest Element by default. Fold claim-strength analysis into Overall Judgment or Core Defect Snapshot, and mention strengths or weaknesses only where they affect the professor's assessment.

## Location Rule

For the Main Problems section, provide concrete locations by default. Use paragraph, page, section, heading, and footnote references that were preserved during extraction. Location references are required unless the submitted material or extraction quality makes them unavailable; in that case, state the limitation explicitly.

## Format Examples for Main Problems Section

These examples show **diagnostic structure and language style**, not real legal content. Adapt flexibly to fit the actual defects found.

### Example 1: Claim-Evidence Gap (Overstating Authority)

**Section 3.1, ¶5, footnote 23**: The paper claims "states are legally obligated to admit climate refugees" but cites only an UNHCR policy brief and two academic articles. Policy recommendations and scholarship are not binding sources of international law. This conflates lex ferenda with lex lata—the claim reaches level 5 (binding obligation) while evidence supports only level 2 (interpretive possibility). **Fatal defect** for the paper's central thesis.

### Example 2: Source Hierarchy Confusion

**Page 18, paragraph 2**: The paper treats a COP decision and an ICJ judgment as equivalent authority when arguing that [specific legal principle]. COP decisions are political instruments without binding force on non-parties, while ICJ judgments create binding obligations for parties to the dispute and strong persuasive authority generally. Equating these sources undermines the legal reasoning. **Serious defect**, reparable by clarifying source hierarchy.

### Example 3: Missing Legal Bridge

**Section 4.2, under "State Practice Analysis"**: The paper jumps from "three states have enacted domestic legislation" to "customary international law has emerged." No analysis of consistency, duration, generality, or opinio juris is provided. Domestic legislation alone does not prove custom without showing state practice accompanied by legal obligation belief. **Serious defect**—the core custom claim lacks required doctrinal steps.

### Example 4: Adverse Authority Not Addressed

**Throughout Chapter 5**: The paper argues that [position X] without addressing the contrary ICJ ruling in [hypothetical case]. That judgment directly rejects the paper's interpretation on similar facts. Ignoring the strongest adverse authority makes the argument one-sided and unpersuasive. **Serious defect**, reparable by engaging the contrary precedent.

### Flexibility Note

The above examples show:
- Precise location referencing
- Claim → Evidence → Gap structure  
- Severity classification
- Direct diagnostic language

**Adapt this structure** to each paper's specific defects. Do not mechanically copy these formats—use them as models for clear, actionable diagnostic writing.
